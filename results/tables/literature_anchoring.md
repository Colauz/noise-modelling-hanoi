## Conclusion

Plausible absolute bias of our smartphones: **between +3.0 and +7.3 dB** (peer-reviewed sources, night excluded).

What this allows and forbids:

- **Allowed**: comparing our places with each other, our hours with each other, and discussing spatial and temporal contrasts. The bias is common to all three devices and cancels in a difference.
- **Forbidden**: stating a regulatory exceedance rate as a fact. The QCVN daytime threshold (70 dBA) falls in the middle of our distribution: a bias of a few dB shifts the exceedance percentage substantially.

### Notes by source

- **Gelb & Apparicio 2019, Applied Acoustics 148:332-343** — Cyclists INSIDE the flow (~1-2 m from vehicles): more exposed than an observer at the kerb. The expected gap is positive.
- **Phan et al. 2010, Applied Acoustics 71(5):479-485** — The ONLY published Hanoi campaign with professional instrumentation. Lden = annual average with +5 dB evening / +10 dB night penalties: mechanically above a daytime level. Value to be confirmed against the PDF.
- **Phan et al. 2010, Applied Acoustics 71(5):479-485** — Our night-time coverage is too thin (n about 10) for this anchor to be informative: it is carried for the record.
- **Institute of Occupational Health and Environment, 12 major Hanoi arteries (as reported by the Vietnamese press)** — Grey literature: secondary source, undocumented protocol. To be used as contextual orientation, never as a primary reference.
